About

The patch history

My first engineering job was holding the flashlight. Here's the patch history:

  1. Version 1.0 At 7 years old, I held the flashlight while my dad swapped the stereo in an old car for a new touch screen one. Once the wiring was right, it came to life. Suddenly the car felt fancy, with a really cool display that could play all kinds of music. I was hooked.
  2. Version 2.0 Moved from wiring to writing code. Now I build software too, with Python, Java, C++, and AI tools like Claude Code and Replit. Getting a program to run still feels like that stereo turning on.
  3. Known issues Everyone keeps telling me entry-level tech is oversaturated. I considered this carefully and decided to ignore it.
  4. Coming soon More building, more breaking, more fixing. When you love technology that's changing faster than it ever has with AI, the only real option is to keep chasing it.
  5. Background processes Basketball, and explaining things to people who didn't ask. More below.

I still think the flashlight job matters more than it looks. It quite literally illuminated my path. Someone has to see clearly before anything gets built, right?

Philosophy

A critical Stoic learning

Every man's life lies within the present; for the past is spent and done with, and the future is uncertain.
Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, Book 3, Section 10

It changed the way I work. The past is already done and the future isn't here yet, so the only thing I can actually act on is the task in front of me. Instead of replaying a bad test or stressing about a deadline weeks away, I put my full attention on the next step. That keeps me calmer and makes me a lot more productive, whether I'm debugging code or planning a lesson.

Southwest tofu burritos Makes 8 · about 12 g protein each · 45 min
Ingredients 0 of 9 ready
Steps
  1. Simmer the rice in 1¾ cups water, covered, for 35 to 40 minutes.
  2. Press the water out of the tofu, crumble it, and toss it with the taco seasoning and lime juice.
  3. Brown the tofu in a non-stick pan over medium-high heat for 6 to 7 minutes, then stir in the beans for a minute or two.
  4. Warm the tortillas, fill each with rice, tofu and beans, salsa, and avocado, then fold in the ends and roll tight.
  5. Optional: crisp the burritos in a hot pan, 3 to 4 minutes a side.
